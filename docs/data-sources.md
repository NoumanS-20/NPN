# Data sources

Every dataset used by either application, with its real provenance. Nothing here is guesswork: each entry
was checked against the source's own description on 25 September 2026, and the SCMS origin was verified
against USAID's published dataset rather than the Kaggle page alone.

Raw data is gitignored. Rebuild it with:

```bash
python scripts/fetch_data.py          # download anything missing
python scripts/fetch_data.py --check  # verify without downloading
python scripts/fetch_data.py --list   # print this register from the code
```

## Provenance tiers

| Tier | Meaning | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Real** | Genuine records from a real organisation | Every accuracy figure we report publicly |
| **Public synthetic** | Published openly, but generated by its author | Demonstrating that a method works; never quoted as real-world behaviour |
| **Generated by us** | Created by our own seeded scripts from the patterns above | Making the problems realistically large; always labelled in the UI |

Metrics are computed on **real** rows only. A test enforces it.

## PR1 — SupplyGuard

| Dataset | Tier | Licence | What it gives us |
|---|---|---|---|
| `scms/SCMS_Delivery_History_Dataset.csv` | **Real** | Unknown on Kaggle; originally published by USAID | 10,324 purchase orders, 2006–2015, 43 countries. 73 vendors × 88 manufacturing sites = 217 supplier entities, 141 of them competing within the largest product group. Promised and actual delivery dates give an 11.5% late rate. Also unit and pack prices, freight cost, weight, shipment mode, 184 items |
| `procurement/procurement_kpi.csv` | **Real** (stated by its author: real, anonymised, 2022–23) | CC0-1.0 | 777 purchase orders with 63 cancelled and 73 partially delivered — our disruption labels — plus defective units and negotiated versus list price |
| `procurement/supplier_order_lines.csv` | **Public synthetic** — the author states it simulates a semiconductor manufacturer | CC BY 4.0 | 517 order lines across 44 suppliers with material-discrepancy events (13.7%), supplier-fault flags, rework cost and criticality. Our quality labels |
| `sap/lfa1, ekko, ekpo, eket, ekbe, mara.csv` | **Public synthetic** — Google Cloud's `cloud-training-demos.SAP_REPLICATED_DATA` | MIT | Genuine SAP table structures with simulated contents. A 2,590-vendor master, 76,362 purchase-order headers, 125,724 items, and 171,380 schedule lines joinable to goods receipts (45.4% late, though only 25 vendors ever transact). Used for the supplier catalogue and to show ERP-shaped ingestion |
| `procurement/supply_chain_data.csv` | **Public synthetic** | CC0-1.0 | 100 rows of lead times, defect rates and manufacturing costs, used to sanity-check our derived ranges |

**Correction on the record:** an earlier draft of our design described the SAP files as a real company's
system export. They are not. They are Google Cloud's public demonstration data. The structures are real SAP;
the contents are simulated, and we say so wherever the dataset appears.

## P2 — TrendWear Planner

| Dataset | Tier | Licence | What it gives us |
|---|---|---|---|
| `retail/retail_store_inventory.csv` | **Public synthetic** — "a synthetic dataset for practicing inventory management and demand forecasting" | CC0-1.0 | 73,100 daily rows; we use the 14,626 Clothing rows. 20 products across 5 stores over two years, with units sold, inventory level, price, discount (0–20%), promotion flag, seasonality, weather and competitor pricing. The discount column is what the markdown elasticity is fitted on |

Because this dataset is synthetic, our forecast accuracy demonstrates that the method works; it is not a
claim about real shopper behaviour. We say that when presenting the number.

## Generated by us

Created by seeded scripts, reproducible, and labelled `origin = "synthetic"` in every table:

- PR1: additional supplier entities to reach ~520, with commercial terms (MOQ, capacity, contract bands)
- PR1: plants, the material requirement plan, and the approved supplier list
- P2: additional styles on a six-week launch calendar, DC-to-store lanes, plant capacity, fabric bill of
  materials, and the merchandising plan

Each derivation is recorded in `docs/assumptions.md` with the formula used.

## The domain mapping we state openly

PR1's use case describes a manufacturer buying raw materials; SCMS is health-commodity procurement. The
structure is the same — a buyer, approved suppliers, materials, plants, purchase orders, promised and actual
deliveries — so we map: **item → material, vendor and manufacturing site → supplier entity, delivery
location → plant.** We explain the mapping rather than hide it behind renamed columns.
